AN INVESTIGATION INTO AI, POLITENESS & ENVIRONMENTAL COST

Two words.
A world
of work.

When we say “thank you” to an AI assistant, it may generate another reply—using computation, electricity and potentially water through the supporting infrastructure.

Yet polite wording inside a useful request can change the answer in a different way. Is courtesy wasteful, helpful, or a question of how we design AI?

Is our politeness
costing the planet?

It sounds like a question about the electricity used by two words. It is really a question about what work those words trigger—and whether that work creates value.

A stand-alone thank-you can start a new AI response. A politely phrased question may instead change the length or quality of an answer you already needed. Those two behaviours are not equivalent. Neither can be evaluated from token counts alone.

ONE MEASURED STARTING POINT 0.245 Wh Reported mean hardware energy per generated thank-you reply

Delavande, Pierrard and Luccioni evaluated generated replies to thank-you messages appended to 10,000 conversation histories adapted from the UltraChat dataset. GPU and CPU energy were instrumented, while RAM energy was estimated. Their reported average comes from a particular Llama 3.1–8B / H100 experiment—not a universal cost for ChatGPT.

It is not a water-consumption measurement. We cannot use an unrelated model provider's water estimate to fill that gap.

ACT 02THE COURTESY PARADOX

Polite. But in what way?

Adding “please” inside useful work is a different intervention from sending “thank you” after the work is done.

2.1 / CROSS-STUDY SYNTHESIS

Separate the interventions

A request with polite wording may alter an answer already needed. A separate acknowledgment may initiate a new response. These two cases cannot be collapsed into a single etiquette rule.

Scroll to continue ↓
2.2 / PUBLISHED GROUP MEANS

Models respond differently

In PromptTone’s Italian coding study, polite requests produced shorter mean code outputs for GPT and Gemini—but longer ones for Claude. The unit here is characters.

Scroll to continue ↓
2.3 / INTERPRETATION / LIMITATION

Do not swap the units

A change in output characters is not a measured change in energy, and it is not proof of improved code quality. Our conclusions have to preserve the study boundary.

ACT 06DESIGN & AUTHORITY

Design the intelligence. Not the manners.

The possible solution is not to shame human courtesy. It is to determine which interactions actually require costly inference—and when accuracy or human value justifies it.

6.1 / DESIGN HYPOTHESES

Three interface choices

A generated reply, a lightweight acknowledgment and silent closure each carry different possible benefits and risks. The research assembled here does not prove which is best.

What does a

ACT 02THE COURTESY PARADOX

More polite.
More efficient?

A separate thank-you is not the same thing as a polite phrase inside a request that would have happened anyway.

In the PromptTone programming benchmark, polite versus neutral phrasing coincided with shorter mean code outputs for GPT and Gemini, but longer ones for Claude. These are differences in characters from a published summary table—not tokens, energy savings or verified code quality.

02 / THE CONTRADICTIONCourtesy's effect depends on the intervention, model and task. A universal rule would misrepresent the evidence.

ACT 03WORK → VALUE

The shortest answer
isn't always the
best answer.

Optimizing the cost of a single message can obscure the cost of an entire failed task.

What we want to measure is the energy required to deliver an acceptable resolution—including corrections and retries. But the available thank-you and PromptTone studies do not provide a compatible, complete set of resolution outcomes and energy measurements. That gap is itself a finding: the metric we most want is not directly identifiable from these studies.

DESIRED METRIC · NOT CALCULATED HERE
Energy per verified resolution = Total task energy / Correctly resolved tasks
Cannot be computed from the available cross-study evidence. A conceptual evaluation target, not a published result.
FRAMEWORK F2AThe Resolution Dividend

Determine whether additional compute improves verified outcomes. Do not confuse first-pass brevity with total effectiveness.

ACT 04LANGUAGE & EQUITY

Same task.
Unequal machinery.

Language is not nationality, and politeness does not explain the entire multilingual energy gap. But the language–energy relationship is measurable.

Deng and colleagues measured inference across 122 languages. In one benchmark, energy per output token varied by as much as 8.3×, with larger total differences driven in part by token generation. Some high-energy languages also had worse benchmark outcomes. This doesn't establish that people using those languages are less capable, or that they are more polite.

ACT 05ENVIRONMENTAL GEOGRAPHY

Every answer
happens somewhere.

The person receiving the answer may be far from the place where electricity is generated or cooling water is consumed.

Google estimated that its median Gemini Apps text prompt consumed 0.24 Wh of energy and 0.26 mL of data-centre cooling water in May 2025. This is a different product, request distribution and accounting boundary from the thank-you experiment; we cannot borrow Google's millilitres to estimate a ChatGPT thank-you.

ACT 06DESIGN & AUTHORITY

Design the intelligence.
Not the manners.

If the interface determines which human signals become computational work, we should evaluate the interface—not merely ask people to stop being polite.

Three possible acknowledgment designs offer different trade-offs. Their relative benefits have not been established by the research assembled here. Select a design to examine its promise, risk and the evidence required before deployment.

A secondary application: wealth-management judgment +

Fictional scenario, not a measured study. An advisor asks an assistant to review a proposed recommendation for portfolio concentration, liquidity and suitability. The assistant's real value is identifying missing evidence and potential concerns—not sounding agreeable or producing the fewest tokens.

Authority remains with qualified professionals and applicable supervisory processes. The lesson travels beyond wealth management: in consequential decisions, resource optimization must not displace independent challenge.

The question is no longer “How much does thank you cost?”
It's “What work did we choose to perform—and was it worth doing?”
