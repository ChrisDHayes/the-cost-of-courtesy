## Data and evidence guardrails

1. **0.245 Wh:** One reported experiment with a particular model/hardware/measurement boundary; not a universal cost per thank-you.
2. **PLUM:** Preprint composite response scores, not verified accuracy, measured inference energy or causal effect of courtesy.
3. **122 languages:** Published language-condition aggregates from one Qwen3–8B benchmark, not average costs for speakers, countries or real-world traffic.
4. **12-language × 3-model table:** A separate published cross-model comparison; not joined to PLUM.
5. **0.24 Wh and 0.26 mL:** Google-scale text-prompt accounting, not transferable to the separate thank-you experiment.
6. **Three interface endings:** Clearly identified design hypotheses; no invented energy or user-satisfaction rankings.

## Light-touch industry implications

The hypothetical wealth-management-dealer example is a **case study in evaluation design**, not a claim about any named firm. Advisors' use of AI for meeting preparation or explanations invites tests of completeness, accuracy, permission boundaries, human oversight, communication clarity and rework—not only tokens saved.

For Data & AI leaders, the general decision is whether a system produces **verified useful outcomes, fairly and with proportionate computation**. That is not a proven optimization outcome in the cited studies; it is a practical research and governance agenda.
