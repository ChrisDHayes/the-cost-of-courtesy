## Editorial verdict
**Ready for author sign-off**, subject to final reading in the Substack editor and any desired Safari/physical-device check.

The piece has a clear through-line:
1. Human acknowledgment is not necessarily a request for further inference.
2. Politeness **inside** a task differs from a standalone thank-you.
3. Evaluated model responses to courtesy vary by language, model and conversational context.
4. Energy per reply is not energy per verified useful outcome.
5. Averages conceal heterogeneous multilingual costs and accuracy; inference costs also differ by model.
6. The facility and water consequences cannot be assigned to a user's language or location without evidence.
7. The design implication is proportionate computation and human-centred closure, not discouraging courtesy.

## Evidence discipline
- **Reported 0.245 Wh** is tied to the paper's specified Llama/H100 response-generation experiment, not a generic ChatGPT reply.
- **PLUM courtesy scores** are the preprint's equally weighted machine-scored composite and are not observed task success, energy, or universally validated language-independent quality.
- **122 language observations** are source language-condition aggregates under one benchmark, not user traffic or per-language constants.
- **12 × 3 cross-model energy matrix** is a separate published comparison in J/output token; one Tibetan condition is explicitly flagged source-censored.
- **0.24 Wh and 0.26 mL** are attached only to the separately bounded Google-scale median text-prompt analysis. No energy-to-water transfer is made between studies.
- The matrices remain separate; no synthetic “politeness cost” index or cross-study causal inference is claimed.
